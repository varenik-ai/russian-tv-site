Moscow Time to EST, CST, MST and PST: A Russian TV Schedule Guide for the USA

Short answer: Moscow is 8 hours ahead of New York in winter and 7 hours ahead in summer (9 and 8 for Chicago, 10 and 9 for Denver, 11 and 10 for Los Angeles). Moscow never changes its clocks (it is on UTC+3 all year); the US does, so the gap changes twice a year. The tables below convert the main hours, and the converter does any time for you.

Why the gap changes

Russia abolished seasonal clock changes, so Moscow stays on UTC+3. The US moves to daylight saving time: the clocks went back on November 1, 2026 and go forward on March 14, 2027 (and back again on November 7, 2027). As a result, in the Eastern time zone the gap is 8 hours in winter (UTC−5) and 7 hours in summer (UTC−4); the other zones shift in the same way.

Exceptions: Arizona, Alaska, Hawaii

  • Arizona (Phoenix) does not change its clocks and stays on UTC−7 all year (like Mountain time in winter): Moscow 21:00 is 11:00 am all year.
  • Alaska (Anchorage) is UTC−9 in winter and UTC−8 in summer: Moscow 21:00 is 9:00 am in winter and 10:00 am in summer.
  • Hawaii (Honolulu) does not change its clocks and stays on UTC−10 all year: Moscow 21:00 is 8:00 am all year.

Planning your day: what the gap means

  • Moscow evening = your lunch or midday. Prime time at 21:00 Moscow is 1:00 pm on the East Coast and 10:00 am on the West Coast in winter, so evening news and shows are daytime viewing in the USA.
  • Moscow morning = your night. Moscow 9:00 is 1:00 am Eastern in winter, so morning programmes are best skipped or watched in the repeat.
  • News at any hour. Rossiya 24 broadcasts round the clock, so there is always something live.
  • Moscow midnight is afternoon. On December 31, Moscow midnight is 4:00 pm Eastern and 1:00 pm Pacific; see our New Year's Eve guide.
  • Watch the day marker. Early Moscow hours fall on the previous evening in the USA (see "previous day" in the tables), so check the date before you plan.

Moscow time in the UK: GMT and BST

Moscow is on UTC+3 all year. The UK is on GMT (UTC+0) in winter and on British Summer Time (UTC+1) in summer. The clocks went back on Sunday, October 25, 2026 and go forward again on Sunday, March 28, 2027. So the gap is three hours in winter and two hours in summer.

The TV Licence: what to know

This is a UK-specific point that many viewers miss. According to TV Licensing, you need to be covered by a TV Licence to watch or record programmes as they are being shown live on any channel or service, whichever device you use and wherever the broadcast comes from, including live streams of channels from outside the UK. Watching only on-demand content does not need one (except BBC iPlayer). If your household already has a licence, it covers you; check the rules and your own situation on the TV Licensing website. Speed and tips

About 5 Mbit/s is enough for HD. If other people in the house stream at the same time, a cable connection or a stronger Wi-Fi point helps. A VPN is not needed and usually adds delay. On mobile data, HD can use roughly one to two gigabytes an hour, so Wi-Fi is better on a capped plan. If a channel does not start, wait a minute, reload or try another one; the player also switches to a backup source on its own. The guide Channel not loading: 8 fixes covers the usual causes.

Planning your viewing

  • After work. The Moscow prime time at 21:00 is 6:00 pm in winter and 7:00 pm in summer, so the evening news and main shows are watchable live on a weekday.
  • At the weekend. Morning and midday Moscow programmes arrive between 6:00 and 10:00 am in the UK, so weekend mornings suit films and children's channels such as Karusel.
  • News at any hour. Rossiya 24 broadcasts round the clock.
  • Holidays. For New Year and other special broadcasts, convert the Moscow start time first; on December 31 Moscow midnight is 9:00 pm in London.

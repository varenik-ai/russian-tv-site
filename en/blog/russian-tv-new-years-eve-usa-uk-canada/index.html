How to Watch Russian TV on New Year's Eve in the USA, UK and Canada (2026)

For millions of Russian-speaking families abroad, New Year's Eve is still watched on Russian television: the countdown to Moscow midnight, the festive concerts, the classic New Year films. The problem for viewers in North America and the UK is the clock: Russia celebrates when it is still afternoon or early evening where you live. This guide gives you the exact times for every time zone, explains what these evenings usually look like on air, and shows how to watch the live channels for free on any device, without a VPN.

When is midnight in Moscow where you live?

Russian national channels run on Moscow time (UTC+3). Russia abolished seasonal clock changes, so Moscow is on UTC+3 all year round: on December 31 the gap to North America is large, and the gap to the UK is exactly three hours. Two practical points. First, the New Year address and the countdown happen at the very end of the Moscow evening, so in the US and Canada you will watch them in the early afternoon of December 31 and can still celebrate again at your own local midnight. Second, because of the eleven-hour gap with the US West Coast, many families plan a "Moscow midnight" lunch or an early family dinner; the stream is a convenient way to be part of it without cable.

What usually airs on New Year's Eve

The exact schedule is published by the channels in the second half of December and can change, so treat the list below as the usual pattern rather than a promise:

  • The New Year address. By long tradition the head of state addresses the country shortly before midnight, and the national channels air it together, followed by the Kremlin chimes and the national anthem. It is the moment most families watch live.
  • Festive concerts and variety shows. The main federal channels fill the evening with long musical programmes with popular singers and actors, such as the "Novogodny Goluboy Ogonyok" music show; the late-evening and night hours repeat or continue the celebration.
  • Classic New Year films. Soviet comedies such as "The Irony of Fate, or Enjoy Your Bath!" and "Carnival Night" are a New Year tradition, and you can also expect new festive films and comedies on the large channels. For classic cinema all year, see Dom Kino.
  • News. News channels keep their programmes: Rossiya 24 continues to broadcast around the clock.

Check the programme before you plan your day: most channel pages on russian-tv.com show a "Today's programme" block where available, and you can also compare with each channel's own announcement. The festive grid for the evening is usually announced in late December.

Prepare a day early

  1. Test on December 30. Open the channel you plan to watch on the device you will use and watch it for a few minutes.
  2. Check your connection. About 5 Mbit/s is enough for HD; if many people stream in your home, use a cable or a stronger Wi-Fi spot.
  3. Update your browser and turn off a VPN: the site works without one, and a VPN often adds delay.
  4. Have a backup channel. If a stream lags, open another one (for example Rossiya 1 instead of Perviy Kanal); the player also switches to a backup source on its own when a source fails.
  5. Expect a small delay. Live streams over the internet are usually a few seconds to half a minute behind cable. Do not count down with an external clock: watch the countdown on the stream.

Midnight in Moscow (UTC+3) is 21:00 UTC. That is 9:00 pm in London, 4:00 pm in New York and Toronto, 3:00 pm in Chicago, 2:00 pm in Denver, 4:00 pm in Winnipeg, 3:00 pm in Edmonton, 2:00 pm in Vancouver (these three Canadian provinces stopped changing clocks in 2026), and 1:00 pm in Los Angeles.

Yes, it is free, with no registration and no VPN. Open russian-tv.com, pick a channel and press play. If you use a VPN and the picture lags, try turning it off.

Will the New Year address be on air? ▾

By tradition the national channels air the New Year address shortly before midnight Moscow time, followed by the Kremlin chimes. The exact festive schedule is announced by the channels in late December, so check the programme closer to the date.

Can I watch on my TV? ▾

Yes. Open russian-tv.com/tv in the TV's browser, or send the stream from your phone or computer to the TV with AirPlay (iPhone) or Cast (Chrome).

Why is the stream a few seconds behind cable? ▾

Live streams over the internet are cut into short segments, which adds a delay of a few seconds to half a minute. This is normal. Watch the countdown on the stream itself instead of an outside clock.

Does it work in the UK and Canada, not only in the USA? ▾

Yes. The channels are available worldwide, and the times above include the UK and every Canadian time zone.
